A story that carries on without you

Goaden and Ashai live in a London that keeps its own clock. They wake, work, argue, eat badly, go out, and get pulled into things — on London time, whether or not anybody is watching. Open it at three in the morning and they are asleep.

A living novel that remembers

Every hour is written into the history of the city. What happened last Tuesday is still true this Tuesday. The characters carry their mornings, their loyalties, and their quiet grievances forward into every conversation. The writing is crafted in advance, and the living world decides which scenes the day has earned.

  • Real time

    One London clock, one weather report, one sunrise. The Chimes of Renewal ring forty-one seconds late, as they have since they were hung.

  • It remembers

    A broken evening costs something the next morning. A favour is still owed a fortnight later. Nothing resets at midnight except the day.

  • Stories with an ending

    Longer threads build over days and finish — sometimes well, sometimes at a price, sometimes with somebody deciding it is not worth it.

  • A city with other people in it

    The Legion rehearse. Road sprites mend things nobody asked them to mend. Lintels drift over the buildings with the best magic. None of it waits for the leads to arrive.

Where it sits

It runs alongside the novel and the webtoon, in the same continuity, at a fixed point early in the story — before the events that would spoil the book. Reading it will not tell you what happens in The Secrets of Silver Clouds. It tells you what a Tuesday is like.
