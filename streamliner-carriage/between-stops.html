THE STREAMLINER / NARRATED SCENE

Between stops.

The Streamliner swept through London, an enchanted train whose silver carriages could slip straight through solid buildings. Inside its maintenance bay, Road Sprites tended the humming machinery, their tiny forms darting between brass pipes and heaps of salvaged parts. These nimble magical scavengers could reach places human hands never would.

Orange dropped through the roof hatch and hit the workbench in a clatter of stolen screws, his boots skidding across the polished brass. A screwdriver spun towards the edge. Shades caught it before it fell, turning slowly towards the grinning Road Sprite.

“Tell me that’s what I asked for.”

Orange hauled a Storm Capacitor from beneath his jacket, its glass casing alive with writhing blue light. Static prickled through his sleeves as he dropped it onto the bench, sending a bright spark snapping against Shades’s glasses.

“Even better, mate.”

Shades rubbed the blackened lens with his cuff. “A Pulse Cell. I said a Pulse Cell.”

“This one’s got more power.”

“So has a bloody lightning strike. Doesn’t mean I want one in the reading lamps.”

Blue snorted from beside the window, one hand resting against the ward gauge. Orange’s grin widened, his glowing eyes already wandering towards the fittings above them.

Outside, London streamed past in ribbons of amber and violet. Arcane markings shimmered along the window frame, their gentle radiance spilling across the little crew. Beneath the carriage, rails of solidified light threaded the city’s crowded heart, carrying the Streamliner towards a wall of soot-dark brick.

Orange scrambled onto the sill, pressing his face to the glass.

“Here we go.”

The building swallowed them. Pale brickwork swept through the cupboards, its ghostly edges washing over Orange’s outstretched fingers. For a breath, the maintenance bay seemed caught inside the city itself, layered windows and distant rooms drifting through warm brass. Then the walls dissolved behind them, leaving the river glittering beyond the glass.

Orange wriggled his fingers at the vanishing bricks, a delighted laugh bursting from his throat.

“Never gets old.”

“Neither do you, apparently,” Shades muttered. “Regulator. Bottom drawer.”

Orange sprang down, landing beside the tray of salvaged components, the thrill of the roof run still humming through his limbs. The rail companies had found plenty of uses for creatures who could race across carriage roofs at full speed.

He wrestled open the drawer, rummaging beneath coils and mismatched screws until a brass ring caught the light.

“This?”

Shades took it, turning its little dial until the capacitor’s crackle softened into an even hum. Blue’s shoulders eased as the wavering light settled, its reflection steady against the gauge.

With the circuit tucked beneath one arm, Shades squeezed through the maintenance vent. His boots kicked against the grille before disappearing into the narrow opening, followed by a muffled curse.

Orange crouched beside it. “You stuck?”

“I’m working.”

“Sounds painful.”

“It will be when I get out.”

Something clicked inside the wall. The workbench lamp flared, shuddered, then went black. Beyond the maintenance door, the reading lights guttered out with it.

Orange’s laughter stopped. Blue glanced from the darkened bench to the ward gauge, its needle holding steady beneath his palm.

“Lamp circuit,” he called into the vent. “You’ve pulled the wrong connection.”

“Yeah. Cheers. Noticed that.”

Orange fumbled for the screwdriver, his earlier grin tightening as he pushed it through the opening. Tiny fingers closed around the handle. He held the loose grille aside, listening to the scrape of metal and Shades’s increasingly personal complaints about whoever had fitted the thing.

A little warmth returned to the bulb.

Gold unfurled across the workbench, caressing the scattered parts. Light spilled back through the passenger doorway as Shades wriggled into the carriage, grey hat dusted with soot, the screwdriver clamped between his teeth.

Orange caught his boots and helped him down.

From beyond the maintenance door came a child’s delighted voice, stumbling through a sentence before trying it again. Orange glanced towards the sound, his fingers loosening around the grille. Through the narrow gap, a mother bent over an open book, her hand guiding a small finger along the page.

He watched them for a moment, warmth spreading beneath his jacket.

Shades nudged his shoulder. “Still reckon we needed more power?”

Orange looked back at the glowing bulb. “Could’ve lit the whole bloody train.”

“Exactly.”

Someone had stopped in the doorway. Shades lifted his glasses, meeting the newcomer’s gaze before nodding towards the spare place at the bench.

“You any good with your hands?”

Orange slid the tray closer, loose components chiming against one another.

“Come on,” he said, his grin returning. “You can help me find a spare Pulse Cell.”
